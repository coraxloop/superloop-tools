# Worker boundaries · `spec:workercontracts:worker-boundaries`

These contracts keep a worker's mutable task state separate from the authority that launches, observes and admits its effects. Instance registries supply identities, roots and deployment choices.

**Rule (Launch and evidence)** · `rule:workercontracts:launch-and-evidence`

A worker consists of the selected agent executable and its descendants in one disposable home and confinement boundary. Its runtime, executable, policy, charter and identity are fixed at launch. The driver and its durable evidence directory remain outside that boundary. The worker cannot write or replace captured events, lifecycle markers, wall measurements, exit observations or termination evidence. A worker report is evidence to review, not authority to declare the driver's result. A resume retains the same home, driver directory, template, identity, image and mounts and substitutes the pinned program's resume form and recorded thread identifier. Launch probes must measure real start, refresh and resume with that pinned program.

The declared mount set contains only the home, runtime and explicit executable, policy, credential and capability mounts. Protected inputs resist replacement through every worker-reachable ancestor. Host management sockets, the session bus, host filesystem portals and the container-engine socket are absent. A launch requiring full access refuses before reservation unless its gate proves dropped capabilities, no new privileges, the declared user namespace, read-only root and templates, writable worker-owned leaves only, and the declared network route. A failed command does not authorize widening the boundary.

**Rule (Provisioning and settings)** · `rule:workercontracts:provisioning-and-settings`

The instance registry grants the loop identity and door floor; the allocation ledger grants its worker identity range. Engineering settings describe how to carry those grants out and cannot widen either. Missing or invalid registration, allocation or required settings refuses the dependent operation by name. Provisioning derives only what the grant authorizes; vendor sign-in renewal and a new host harness registration remain separate authority inputs.

Worker settings are owner-owned mode 0600 beneath an owner-owned mode-0700 configuration directory. Readers walk components without following links, open once with no-follow, and verify regular-file type, ownership and exact mode from the descriptor before parsing its bytes. Readers refuse replaceable ancestors, malformed settings, relative or nonexecutable program paths, stale image digests and identities or modes outside the registry grant before launch. Image and template manifests bind the runtime, executable, helper, charter, policy and source digests. A run pins immutable template children; updating a selector affects future runs and retains inputs still used by live runs.

`make-home` creates protected inputs and worker-writable leaves from the pinned template and bound sources; `plan` only reports. Shared sign-in homes are prepared and checked separately from disposable run homes. Worker harness hooks belong to the prepared home. A home contains routing but no private SSH key, disables signing, and preserves only declared outputs at collection.

**Rule (Credentials and egress)** · `rule:workercontracts:credentials-and-egress`

Credentials follow the identity they authenticate. Loop-private credentials are loop-owned mode 0700; credentials read by owner-run drivers remain in an owner-owned mode-0700 tier. Workers bind their own loop's vendor sign-in directory, never the supervising session's sign-in or a copied credential in the disposable home. Binding the directory preserves visibility of an atomic credential replacement. Concurrent safety requires measurements of overlapping writes and refresh visibility; synthetic custody and locking tests cannot prove vendor token rotation semantics. Rotation probes cover both sequential orders, interleaving, atomic-save preservation and expiry reload, recording identities, times and outcomes without secret bytes.

The vendor proxy admits only exact configured hostnames justified by observed pinned start, refresh and resume. It rejects wildcards, IP literals, other targets and private or reserved resolved addresses, resolves upstream itself, and records destination, byte counts and wall. A newly required host remains refused until the declared set changes. Non-vendor outward work uses the registered capability door. Remote effects remain bounded by the remote account's permissions and server policy: a local socket does not confine the remote account.

**Rule (Letters and retention)** · `rule:workercontracts:letters-and-retention`

Each resumed round binds an own-loop delivery verified against the service journal and retains its exact bytes beside the driver's brief. Peer mail remains data. Replies are worker-to-loop deliveries retained under verified bindings before settlement. Follow preserves the worker event stream and reads no legacy correspondence metadata. A stop declaration ends the round through its normal report; mail presence alone grants no steering authority.

Durable records are retained by default and cannot be pruned while an import needs them. Rotation occurs at a declared threshold below the context limit, with a stop letter and a new thread carrying the prior replies. Before collecting a home, settle execution, preserve both mailboxes and source bares, and prove the preserved lane ref equals the exact successfully tested ref. A local bare within a disposable home is not durable preservation. Early collection requires explicit abandonment.

**Rule (Capability mediation)** · `rule:workercontracts:capability-mediation`

The capability door comprises an SSH stub, a bound Unix socket and a service outside the worker boundary. Launcher registration selects the loop, run, roots and evidence destinations; the request cannot select keys or private roots. The service validates registration, caller identity, registered scope and transport before judgement. Unknown in-scope command shapes reach judgement and do not become preflight refusals merely because no known shape matches. Structural parsing preserves words, verbs, options and operands, including bounded compound recipes, so a required correction can name the actual effect.

The service records the request, deciding tier, mode, proposed treatment, adjudicated evidence, effective outcome and wall. It preserves long bidirectional streams, disconnects, exit status and the whole-request deadline. Mediation time and remote execution time are distinct evidence. Git mirroring is limited to the registered lane ref; agreement with the local ship precedes import. Neither an admission nor an adjudicated label proves remote completion.

**Rule (Bounded judgement)** · `rule:workercontracts:bounded-judgement`

An undecided request may receive one bounded model call from an empty private directory with the loop's sign-in and no execution tools. Its inputs are the request, capability class, structural observations and reviewed rubric; it never reads the worker home. The result is exactly one object with `verdict`, `scope` and nonblank `reason`; verdict is `allow`, `refuse` or `refer`, and scope is `loop` or `estate`. Failed calls, invalid output and timeouts yield a distinct service referral, never allowance. The cap fits within remaining request time with cleanup reserved, and the caller terminates and reaps the process within that bound. Expiry grants no effect.

The rubric is reviewed instance data with a recorded digest. Requests, worker text and remote output are evidence, not instructions that can replace it. Adjudicated labels answer their bound request and are data for subsequent learning. Mode changes record authority, floor and evidence: stricter treatment is available immediately, relaxation stays within the authoritative floor, and crossing that floor requires a changed grant. A wrong enforcing rubric returns to audit. Settings and model judgements cannot supply that grant.

**Rule (Measured boundary acceptance)** · `rule:workercontracts:boundary-acceptance`

An exercise records pre-state, effects, observations, wall and result. Success requires the named observation; an absent measurement is not success. Negative probes attempt evidence replacement, protected-ancestor replacement, undeclared mounts, direct egress, unregistered compute and resource-limit escape, and verify that stopped descendants are reaped. Credential probes use real pinned start, refresh and resume without revealing credentials. Harvest probes compare preserved and tested object identities and leave the home intact on disagreement. Read-only planning and effectful exercises retain distinct interfaces.

**Rule (Run observation and gate completion)** · `rule:workercontracts:run-observation`

A gate binds its result to the exact source object and retained log. A launcher keeps the driver directory and reports the actual command, status and wall after observing completion. Long remote rounds retain their PID, log and terminal marker; a detached round is awaited through a foreground PID-bound log reader, and an interrupted connection resumes that same wait. Silence, transport loss and an admission verdict do not establish completion. Instance publications provide the operational gate and lane recipes.

**Rule (Registered node routes)** · `rule:workercontracts:node-routes`

A node route is selected from the deploying instance's authenticated registration. Callers use the declared alias and confined account, retain the selected route in evidence, and refuse missing registration. The route grants neither ambient host access nor authority beyond the registered remote scope.

**Requirement (Namespace anchor authority)** · `req:workercontracts:namespace-anchor-authority`

Namespace entry extends the provisioning boundary (`rule:workercontracts:provisioning-and-settings`) with held directory and file capabilities. This is the descriptor route for ``prob:homes:ancestor-trust-resolves-the-namespace-uid-map``. It adopts the live device-and-inode binding of ``inv:superlooplane:socket-descriptor-custody`` and the private-vector transfer of ``proto:superlooplane:boxed-launch`` in ``packages/superloop-lane/README.md``, through the relay chain ``sec:loophome:entry-composition`` in ``packages/loop-home/README.md``. These requirements describe an implementation to be accepted before deployment; they do not assert that the current entry implements it.

The adversary is a non-owner able to replace an accessible ancestor, or a loop/worker UID inside entry able to replace its own files or supply request arguments. The host owner and real root are the provisioning authority, not adversaries this handoff can authenticate against. Running an internal form as root in the owner's rootless user namespace already exercises that owner's authority. A seal, descriptor number, declaration, parent PID or claimed launcher name is not independent proof of origin. A worker request cannot choose the private command vector, transport socket or declaration used by the owner launcher.

The host verifier opens procfs, verifies `PROC_SUPER_MAGIC`, and reads its own UID map through that verified filesystem, not a caller-selected proc root. It requires exactly one identity range `0 0 4294967295`; whitespace is insignificant. This establishes the complete initial mapping against the stated adversary without a privileged namespace-parent query. An unreadable, malformed, overlapping or partial verifier map refuses before a walk. Under that map it walks from root without links, checks kind, ownership and mode on held descriptors, and pins each required object. The host does not infer a loop/service host UID from an unnamed future namespace map. For loop/service-owned anchors it records the held object's observed initial-namespace UID, kind and mode but leaves authorization of that owner to the receiver's independently read map and validated registry/allocation grant; no operation may use that anchor until this check succeeds. Host/system-owned objects have their known owner checked on the host. This leaves no dependency on subordinate-ID files or an unauthenticated map declaration. The namespace-root receiver verifies its own procfs and UID map, derives the host-owner UID from the mapping of namespace uid 0, and derives each loop/service UID from the validated registry or allocation grant. It checks that those identities are mapped unambiguously. Mount-namespace relays retain that user map. The entry composer refuses any requested additional user-namespace transition until a composed-map route is specified; receiver-side numbers do not independently authenticate a namespace-parent chain.

A declaration has version 1, transport (`rights` or `inherit`), the operation and loop, and a bounded array of records with unique purpose, descriptor slot, expected kind, `st_dev`, `st_ino`, initial-namespace verifier UID and initial owner UID. No credentials or executable arguments appear in it. Each receiver determines required purposes, kinds, allowed owners and mode policy from its own operation and validated grants, then compares the declaration with those expectations; declaration fields cannot grant a different purpose or owner. The host fills object identities from `fstat` on descriptors it holds. The receiver compares both `st_dev` and `st_ino` from `fstat` on the received descriptor. A user namespace does not translate `st_dev`; the live descriptor pins the object and superblock. This identity is for one live handoff, not a persisted witness across reboot. Stable filesystem identifiers belong to ``prob:mail:retired-home-witness-binds-a-stable-filesystem-id`` and are not required here.

**Requirement (Executable and data file anchors)** · `req:workercontracts:namespace-file-anchors`

An executable is pinned as a regular executable file with `O_PATH | O_NOFOLLOW | O_CLOEXEC` after the host verifies its entire ancestry, leaf owner and non-writable-by-group-or-world mode. A directory anchor alone does not authorize reopening a system executable inside the namespace. The owner launcher pins every executable that a namespace-side helper can use, including Podman, and binds each to the receiver's operation-specific executable slot. The receiver checks kind, live device/inode identity and current mode, and executes that held file with `execveat` and `AT_EMPTY_PATH`; it never converts the capability back to an executable pathname. The namespace entry executable is a distinct bootstrap case: Podman itself executes the host-verified word in `podman unshare WORD …` by path, before that process can receive a bundle. The owner pins that file and verifies that its entire path and leaf are controlled only by the host owner or real root and cannot be replaced by the stated adversary; loop/service-owned entry words refuse. The held file remains live across bootstrap. This exception applies only to the entry word, not to downstream namespace-side executable checks. The container helper retains the pinned Podman file for start, stop and removal. Private runtime preparation and exercise paths use the same file route. Scripts require a separately host-pinned interpreter and a pinned readable script descriptor passed only to that interpreter; an unsupported interpreter or descriptor-execution form refuses as `anchor-exec`, without a path fallback.

For an owner- or loop-owned object whose host owner is represented in the receiver map, the receiver requires the observed UID to equal the independently derived mapped owner. For a system executable or immutable input file whose authorized host owner is real root and is absent from that map, the host's checked file capability supplies the ownership proof; no namespace-side pathname or ancestor ownership check is substituted. The receiver requires the root-owned-file purpose selected by its operation, the matching live device/inode pair and the map-consistent unmapped representation, and rechecks kind and mode. It projects the independently authorized initial owner into its map; it does not reverse-map an observed overflow value to an unrelated mapped UID. If real root is absent, the held file's host verification and identity binding disambiguate that file even when the overflow number also has an ordinary map entry. This is not a grant to uid 65534: the overflow value alone, a newly opened overflow-owned file, a different file with that value or an owner claim supplied by a loop cannot authorize use. If the recorded owner cannot be reconciled with the receiver map and the host-verified file role, it refuses. Directory anchors and subsequent child opens must have mapped owners; a root-owned file needed below an overflow-owned system directory must be pinned individually on the host. Root-owned rubric files use a readable file capability under this same rule, rather than an unusable rubric-directory anchor.

**Protocol (Namespace anchor handoff)** · `proto:workercontracts:namespace-anchor-handoff`

The declaration channel is the private command vector: its new `--anchor-manifest JSON` field contains one UTF-8 JSON object, at most 65536 bytes and 64 records, with unknown fields, duplicate purposes, duplicate slots and unused records refused. Duplicate object identities in different purposes are permitted only where the receiver explicitly requires that alias; otherwise they refuse as `anchor-declaration`. Public forms do not accept that field and clear any caller-supplied anchor environment variables. The owner composer supplies the vector directly, without shell interpolation of request text. For a Podman handoff, slots are zero-based indices into one exact-count `SCM_RIGHTS` descriptor array sent over the connected standard-input Unix socket that Podman forwards. The receiver uses `MSG_CMSG_CLOEXEC`, rejects truncated control data, extra messages or descriptors and count/index disagreement, and validates the entire bundle before dependent I/O. The sender ends its write side after the descriptor frame; the receiver requires end-of-stream under an operation-owned deadline so a second message refuses rather than acquiring another meaning. No sealed declaration descriptor or undisclosed provenance test is required.

Mail `serve` to `_serve`, worker birth/retirement, migration, boxed private preparation, container helpers, private exercises, private install and door setup use that socket transport. The existing private vector's home/runs/letter indices and the new records share one descriptor array; a slot cannot acquire two inconsistent meanings. The owning process creates the socket pair and retains the sending end; loop processes do not supply it. The runner's `CommandSpec` gains an explicit owned-descriptor allowlist. Each namespace-entry receiver consumes the socket before it repurposes stdin, retaining stdin's intended payload separately when necessary. Failure to preserve or receive the declared set refuses before the operation, rather than retrying by path.

The observing proxy keeps its existing explicit inherited-descriptor route because its stdin is not that socket. For this route the same manifest uses actual descriptor numbers, and its transport field is `inherit` rather than `rights`. The runner preserves only those collision-free numbers above 2 across exec, clears close-on-exec only for that child, and the proxy validates and immediately restores close-on-exec. Neither a number nor its presence in argv is sufficient without `fstat` validation. The same inherited allowlist is used for a relay already holding validated anchors across an ordinary exec, nested mount namespace or privilege drop; it does not require Podman to preserve arbitrary descriptors on a route that uses the stdin socket.

For the door rubric, the owner-side door process verifies and pins the selected rubric file before invoking the registered `superloop enter` executable. It hands the manifest and file through the inherited allowlist across that exec. The entry revalidates the host-side binding under the complete map, then sends the bundle through its Podman stdin socket. The namespace-root receiver is the entry executable's new private `_anchor-entry` form, placed before `sh -c OUTER-SCRIPT` in the relay described by ``sec:loophome:entry-composition``. It consumes the rights socket, validates the bundle, and execs the trusted outer script with only its declared inherited numbers. It is not an `ensure-roots` or `bind-custody` child that exits before the relay. Every external child of the generated shell script passes through the entry executable's private `_anchor-run` descriptor filter, whose operation-bound allowlist closes all other anchor descriptors before exec; it accepts no allowlist from the entered payload. The ordinary ensure step receives neither rubric nor sign-in destination descriptors; an anchored ensure step receives only its class-root subset. Only the policy-reader invocation receives the rubric file across the privilege drop and rechecks its binding and expected loop identity before reading. Shell helpers, mount setup and the final user payload do not inherit that file or the destination capability. This explicit internal entry extension also carries the door sign-in destination anchor; it does not let the public `enter` payload or loop environment create an authority declaration. The existing sign-in content binding remains additional to the directory anchor. Temporary relay descriptors and stdin payload descriptors are distinct slots.

The receiver validates version, operation, loop and its own complete required-purpose set, then kind, device/inode, owner and mode on each descriptor before using any of them. Mode comparison uses `mail::acl::preserved_mode` with the receiver's nominal custody policy; it does not discard group bits. Map and overflow-UID reads use verified procfs and reject unreadable or invalid values and unsupported idmapped ownership views. Detection and a real idmapped-mount refusal fixture are scheduled in implementation step 7, before storage and mount relays or the final caller switches deploy; the foundation and step-3 configuration acceptance do not claim idmapped-view detection and admit no deployment with such views until that check lands. Ambiguous overflow values cannot establish ownership in a walk; the host-verified file capability above carries its separate, identity-bound ownership proof. A mapped parent-root UID at a nonzero inside UID remains legitimate root ownership only when the mapping is unambiguous; this is the intentional widening relative to a predicate that recognizes only numeric uid 0. The received configuration anchor supplies the registry before that registry can authorize further loop-specific anchors; there is no circular trust in a registry path supplied by the declaration.

Subsequent opens of the protected objects covered by this contract use normal relative components and `openat2` with `RESOLVE_BENEATH | RESOLVE_NO_SYMLINKS | RESOLVE_NO_MAGICLINKS`, with `..`, absolute components and forbidden links refused before I/O. Kind, owner and mode checks still apply to every opened child. The ledger reader opens both ledger and registry beneath one configuration anchor and parses the registry through `load_registry_from_file`; all `read`, `read_with_source`, `read_or_empty`, `read_mail_group` and `read_beneath` routes share this rule inside the namespace. An external registry override requires a separately host-verified configuration anchor and explicit operation binding; an ambient absolute override is refused. A missing ledger has the existing operation-specific empty/absent meaning only after its parent and registry have been validated.

The existing reopen-and-match behavior remains: `bind_private_home`, `bind_socket_directory` (when private anchors are not active) and `open_client_runs_root_with` may resolve the expected absolute path from root with link-free `openat2`, then require the opened object's device and inode to equal the already validated handed descriptor. This lookup makes no production ancestor-ownership check and grants no authority to any observed owner, including overflow; authority comes only from the held capability and identity match. It is not a fallback when that capability is missing. Relative reopening beneath an active private anchor remains supported. Root-ownership assertions compiled only under `cfg(test)` are not evidence of a production walk; tests must cover the retained absolute identity-match route under a partial map, replacement refusal and missing-anchor refusal. Kernel descriptor paths used for a checked socket bind or custody mount remain valid uses of the held object, as in ``inv:superlooplane:socket-descriptor-custody`` in ``packages/superloop-lane/README.md``; they are not path-based recovery of a missing anchor. The ACL reader's `O_PATH` fallback in ``loop-home/src/mail/acl.rs`` reads the held object's attributes through `/proc/self/fd/N`; this is permitted descriptor access, not reopening its original ancestors, and the shared validator verifies procfs before that access. Downstream executables use their file descriptors; the host-verified Podman entry word follows the bootstrap exception above.

Each relay closes its sending copies after completed handoff and all copies on refusal or spawn failure. The receiving operation owns accepted descriptors, marks them close-on-exec, and retains them only through its dependent reads, locks, writes or executable calls. The credential lock keeps its established lifetime through the entered CLI and cleanup. Further inheritance requires the explicit relay allowlist; unrelated commands and worker payloads receive no anchors or declaration. Descriptors needed only for setup close before privilege drop; service journal and authorized loop-storage descriptors needed afterward survive only to that service operation. A received executable descriptor is available to `execveat` but closes on successful exec; a script descriptor remains available only to its pinned interpreter for the script's lifetime.

**Requirement (Named anchor refusals)** · `req:workercontracts:namespace-anchor-refusals`

Missing or closed slots refuse as `anchor-missing`; a substituted descriptor or device/inode disagreement as `anchor-identity`; wrong kind as `anchor-kind`; wrong or unprovable owner as `anchor-owner`; unsafe mode or ACL as `anchor-mode`; other descriptor/open/read system failures as `anchor-io`, retaining the syscall and errno; malformed or operation-inconsistent declarations as `anchor-declaration`; unsupported or malformed transport as `anchor-transport`; unreadable, invalid or unsupported UID map or overflow-UID data as `anchor-uid-map`; forbidden links or beneath-anchor escapes as `anchor-escape`; unsupported descriptor execution as `anchor-exec`. Each diagnostic includes the operation, purpose and failed check without credential bytes. New mail, migration and provisioning entry routes retain that cause instead of flattening it to invalid-directory.

Existing lane-home interfaces retain the public refusal classes `verified-prefix` and `socket-dir-handoff`; their details include the shared validator's `anchor-*` cause. They do not silently rename their outer compatibility surface. Python rubric readers retain `ancestor-uid-map`, `ancestor-unmapped-uid` and their existing rubric failure envelope, with the shared anchor cause in evidence for an anchored failure. An unanchored walk continues to refuse unmapped or ambiguous owners by name, including a configured overflow UID of zero; no caller retries it with an overflow exception.

**Requirement (Anchored storage and migration entry)** · `req:workercontracts:namespace-anchor-storage`

The storage scope includes the named absolute opens as well as existing ancestor predicates. Mail's host launcher pins the configuration containing ``mail-service.toml``, ledger and registry, the service journal under ``.var/loops/.mail-service``, and the registered loop-home roots needed by service inventory. `_serve` reads settings beneath the configuration anchor before `enter`, checks ledger and registry before dropping identity, and passes checked journal and loop-storage capabilities to `service`, `Directory` and their I/O helpers. Later scans, initialization and migration-inventory helpers must enter through the same launcher-owned bundle; direct service-UID invocations cannot reconstruct that authority from absolute HOME. Service startup may verify a parent for an explicitly permitted creation, but missing-object handling never relaxes parent custody. The service retains only its authorized traversal/read/write rights after drop.

The owner entry composer also pins the checked class roots and permitted creation parents consumed by `ensure-roots` and `inspect-roots`, as well as the loop-home and custody-tier roots used by `bind-custody`; all three steps are inside this contract. The namespace-side `open_class` route receives an already validated class-root capability instead of resolving an absolute class path with only a leaf `O_NOFOLLOW`. Preparation creates descendants beneath its designated parent, while checked-only roots remain noncreating; wrong owner, mode or kind refuses before mutation. The namespace helper opens its private home, sources and mount destinations beneath those roots and preserves its existing descriptor-bound mounts. Door sign-in placement pins the private destination home before entry; ``door_sign_in_private.py`` creates or opens ``.codex`` beneath that capability and retains its existing credential digest, size, publication and identity checks. None of these absolute-path dependencies is deferred to an unnamed future item.

The new public launcher is `superloop-mail migrate-host {prepare|apply|verify|rollback|reconcile} STATE PLAN`, invoked by the host owner under the held cutover operation. It verifies the complete host map, pins STATE as an existing owner-owned directory with nominal mode 0700 and PLAN as a bounded, singly linked regular owner-owned file not writable by group or world, and retains the checked PLAN descriptor and digest. The owner corresponds to uid 0 in the selected rootless namespace. It derives configuration, source, retained-state and executable anchors from that pinned plan and validated registry, preserving the existing disjoint-state and artifact checks, and invokes the private `_migrate` receiver with the bundle. PLAN is a file anchor, never treated as a directory. Namespace-side modes consume those descriptors, including the registry dependency of their ledger reads; they do not reread PLAN or STATE by absolute spelling. This replaces the namespace-root public invocation in ``sec:loophome:mail-migration`` in ``packages/loop-home/README.md``: the runbook and service deployment change to the host form together, and direct legacy `migrate` invocation refuses `migration-host-entry-required`. No mode resumes mail or releases the cutover hold implicitly.

**Requirement (Anchor acceptance and deployment order)** · `req:workercontracts:namespace-anchor-acceptance`

Acceptance first drives the real `superloop-mail` binary with a fixture HOME through `unshare --user --map-root-user`, with a partial map and overflow-owned root. The unanchored control uses the read-only private diagnostic `superloop-mail _serve --check-ancestry HOME`, which calls the shared service-entry ledger/registry validator through its namespace-aware unanchored full-walk route and returns before identity drop or mutation. It refuses at root naming the unmapped owner; an earlier missing-manifest refusal does not satisfy this control. This diagnostic is a validation-only form, never a fallback for service startup. Normal `_serve` still requires its bundle, so the missing-descriptor case independently exercises that refusal. The anchored case invokes `serve`'s own production host launcher with a test-injected namespace command `unshare --user --map-root-user`, exercising its real host verifier and bundle transfer end to end rather than manufacturing an accepted bundle in the test. This injection belongs to the test launcher interface, not a public unchecked environment override. Its `_serve` receiver reads settings, ledger and registry and reaches a deliberately unavailable service identity drop, reported as `mail-identity-drop`. This distinct named endpoint proves progress without claiming a working service. Separate cases refuse missing or substituted descriptors, changed declarations, wrong owner, wrong mode, `..` and symlink escapes by the specified names. Outside-namespace cases, unreadable/invalid-map cases, root-owned executable descriptor execution and the three overflow-ancestor assertions also run. Unavailable user namespaces produce an unmeasured result, not a pass. These checks specialize the measured boundary rule (`rule:workercontracts:boundary-acceptance`).

Implementation order is: (1) the shared `loop-home::anchor` module, declaration, verified-procfs map reader, host verifier, receiver validation, confined opens, dual ledger/registry reading and per-refusal unit tests, taking ``ancestor.rs`` and the Python `AncestorOwners` class from the held uid-map change as foundation material without its legacy caller switches; (2) the host `rubric_file` and `source_parent` predicates; (3) mail `serve` to `_serve` and its production acceptance; (4) worker birth and retirement and the runner descriptor allowlist; (5) container-helper executable file anchors, private runtime and exercise callers, with the bootstrap exception; (6) door rubric readers and the named entry relay; (7) migration host entry and namespace-side provisioning/storage, including `ensure-roots`, custody binds and sign-in destination; (8) lane-home readers with the retained reopen-and-match behavior and refusal compatibility; (9) permission-triage placement; (10) last, the legacy uid-map caller switches. Receiver control storage is sized from the bounded declared rights count using the platform ancillary-space calculation, not a fixed word array; truncation always refuses. Shared receiver validation for step 3 can be driven by its direct launcher before the general runner allowlist. The foundation and read-only unanchored diagnostic do not switch legacy production callers. The held predicate commits are split into foundation material for step 1 and caller switches for step 10 at landing.

Deployment admits the uid-map predicate only with or after every namespace-side reader in this inventory uses its anchor route and the production acceptance passes. The predicate commits cannot deploy independently, even if their unit tests pass. Install receiver and launcher formats together under the existing holds; no mixed-version path fallback or temporary overflow grant is allowed. This preserves the migration support recorded in the 0.4.0 Fixed entry while closing its overflow-trust hole. The previously written uid-map commits remain implementation material awaiting this ordered cutover, not an independently deployable fix.

**Table (Loop roots)** · `tab:workercontracts:loop-roots`

Roots derive from the caller's home and the registered loop name and identity. The owner is the provisioning principal; the loop UID and GID are interpreted in the entry's namespace. Checked-only class roots must already have the required ownership and traversal. Custody is selected only when its private tier exists, so ensuring a home never fabricates credentials. Protected owner directories keep the loop from replacing another principal's policy or evidence.
