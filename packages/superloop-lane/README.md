# The lane command

**Definition (Lane command)** · `def:superlooplane:lane-command` `superloop-lane` describes and starts the worker in one disposable home, builds its pinned image, and installs passive launch inputs; the durable driver commands share its dispatcher. The protected plan binds the boxed backend, image checks require that vendor's configured and measured program, and the private entry carries the selection to `driver start|resume --backend codex|claude`.

**Definition (Plan and launch stages)** · `def:superlooplane:plan-and-launch-stages` The plan is a launch contract for review and fixtures. `build` runs the container program on the calling owner's host and records the resulting image; `install` prepares a loop without activating it; `launch` creates the home and starts the driver, which supervises the container and saves evidence in the durable driver directory outside the home.

**Rule (Candidate plan measurements)** · `rule:superlooplane:candidate-plan-measurements` The plan records configured paths, mounts, and runtime options as candidates. Its measurement fields mark values that still need inspection on the launch host, including runtime-created mounts, image and socket inputs, template contents, and the worker UID allocation.

**Requirement (Launch model selection)** · `req:superlooplane:launch-model-selection` Launch selects model and reasoning effort from `Model:` and `Effort:` (or `Reasoning effort:`) in the letter's opening lines, then from `--model` and `--effort` for either missing field. A launch without both values refuses before reservation. The selected values and their sources enter the launch plan, reservation and protected launch evidence; the runtime vector passes them to Codex as `-m` and `-c model_reasoning_effort=…`.

**Protocol (Journal-bound resumed mail)** · `proto:superlooplane:resumed-letter-placement` The loop submits an ordinary letter through its own outbox to its worker. After service delivery, `bind-mail LOOP RUN --step STEP --delivery UTC_INSTANT` verifies the completed journal record, own-loop sender, selected worker, recipient inode and whole-file digest. The service pin becomes durable before the reservation gains its `mail` binding. `launch LOOP RUN --round STEP` re-verifies that same inbox delivery and requires ordinary resume authorization. Each round takes its own `--budget` in seconds (1 through 10,800), defaults to 3,600 when omitted, and does not inherit the dispatch budget. Its prompt names `/mail/inbox/SENDER.DELIVERY.letter`; the driver retains the exact bytes as step evidence beside the brief before starting the resumed turn. A moved or changed inbox entry refuses rather than selecting another file. Model and effort remain those of the reservation. UTF-8 briefs retain the optional model, effort and cooperative-stop declaration convention; binary deliveries remain opaque and are preserved exactly. `send-letter` and `withdraw-letter` are retired commands; a delivered instruction is corrected by another delivery. Old `letters` reservations require cutover reconciliation.

Helper shutdown unlinks only its own control socket, joins the accepted control read, then answers every queued connection until accept would block before dropping the listener. An invalid queued peer does not prevent the remaining peers from being answered; its error is still reported. No new connection can enter the removed pathname, and the existing per-connection read bound remains. Replacement sockets are retained (``test:unit:helper-close-answers-every-queued-control``; ``test:unit:helper-service-cleanup-retains-replacement-sockets``).

A failed helper stop handshake is followed by a wait for the launcher's exact child, within the existing 90-second cancellation bound. A successful waited exit is sufficient to continue settlement; a non-zero exit, wait error or timeout refuses with both errors retained. The helper end is recorded from that same child's waited status. The control models clean acknowledgement, reset then success, reset then non-zero exit, and reset with a live child; its expired deadline selects the timeout branch without an elapsed-time assertion (``test:unit:helper-stop-waits-for-exit-after-handshake-failure``).

A resumed round left `awaiting-settlement` after a `helper-stop:` error is admitted by `launch LOOP RUN --round STEP --dry-run` only with a recorded completed driver end. Apply the returned interrupted-settlement digest with the identical command and `--expect-digest`. Existing process absence, collected-scope, attempt, plan and source binding checks remain required. An observer proven absent by the identity-bound collected-scope proof keeps that provenance; it is not recorded as a successful exit. A reply settles normally and the next round resumes; without a reply recovery marks the round failed, public `settle` completes it, and the next launch requires an explicit thread choice (``test:integration:public-helper-stop-recovers-resumed-reply``; ``test:integration:public-helper-stop-recovers-resumed-no-reply``; ``test:unit:awaiting-round-recovery-requires-completed-driver``; ``test:unit:recovered-observer-needs-collected-identity-proof``).

The first-dispatch `launch --letter` form still refuses a resumed round's retained mail at its loop-owned parent. The barrier cases preserve reservation bytes and inventory; they do not claim that route recovers a resumed round. The fixture seeds its initial settled dispatch and does not prove recovery of a raced first dispatch (``test:integration:public-helper-stop-recovery-refuses-retained-letter-with-reply``; ``test:integration:public-helper-stop-recovery-refuses-retained-letter-without-reply``).

Helper reconstruction reads a resumed round’s retained `mail-STEP.txt` only at the exact path derived from its active step, through the held runs root and the plan’s loop identity. The single matching reservation binding must name the worker and match the bounded file’s bytes and whole-file digest; the regular file keeps the binding reader’s owner, mode, link and no-symlink checks. The runs root remains namespace-owner controlled; the run remains loop-owned. The initial launch letter and initial rotation source retain their separate readers. The read-only helper preflight receives the same custody bundle as service startup, reconstructs the prospective round in memory, and checks the selected mail, helper plan, image, program pins and attempt vector. The request occupancy claim is only for the final name; the preflight’s process-specific temporary is not the inspector’s future temporary. It starts no serving helper, publishes no credentials and consumes no round name. Its report leaves the busy lease, scope admission and preparing-record size before publication, every step after the preparing reservation is published, launch-time descriptor transfer and intervening changes to launch; a passing preview is not a guarantee against those later failures. That publication window includes private inspection and its environment/request writes, stale-socket checks, helper identity publication, credential-authority startup and refresh, and driver startup. The changing launch runs the same preflight before it publishes the preparing round, so a refusal there leaves the reservation settled. Live pre-intent rollback is described below; crash recovery and old stranded-run recovery remain separate work (``test:unit:retained-round-mail-requires-exact-source-and-binding``; ``test:unit:retained-round-mail-keeps-leaf-custody``; ``test:crate:built-helper-resume-retains-mail-custody``).

Every preflight reconstruction, including received-custody identity validation, uses the prospective next-round reservation. It never reads the preceding dispatch letter, rotated brief or prior step’s retained mail. The built-command fixture alters or removes those preceding sources after first-helper readiness and requires both resumed readiness and the built private preflight to pass without changing the run. Vendor execution is synthetic; private inspection, descriptor transfer, plan reconstruction and the helper’s passed report are real built receivers. The public resumed-launch library entry now has a positive dry-run acceptance with canonical production mail birth, provisioning, delivery and binding, a complete synthetic installed runtime, and the real built helper preflight. It compares the raw reservation and run-tree inventory before and after the preview and traces the private helper invocation; image inspection and vendor execution are declared stand-ins, and this preview starts no worker or work scope (`public_resumed_preview_uses_canonical_custody`; ``test:crate:built-helper-resume-retains-mail-custody``).

The boxed entry probe compares the selected `entry_bin`'s `driver-launch --interface-version` answer with the workspace package version and loop-home's driver argument interface 1. It does not probe `lane_bin`, the separate private receiver, and neither number identifies the failed-round settlement protocol. Before deploying that protocol, stop boxed launches and settlements, let old launches finish, replace every host and private lane copy, and compare each copy's hash with the build record before releasing the hold. A future private-receiver capability handshake must cover dispatch, resume and settlement; increasing the unrelated driver argument number alone would not enforce this floor.

If preparing publication itself fails, the launcher reads the reservation again while still holding the mailbox lock. Exact unchanged bytes produce `unchanged: preparing reservation was not published`, not `retained:`; a failed read-back or changed bytes still take the normal rollback path, including a rename followed by a failed directory sync (``test:unit:round-preparing-publication-refuses-without-retaining-unchanged-run``).

The complete resumed-host fixture runs the same public library entry in a real delegated transient work scope. Its loop-uid driver stand-in sends a real framed helper request, binds a production mail reply and lets the host settle; kernel PID, peer UID and work-cgroup checks preserve the host/namespace views. The first dispatch is seeded settled history, including its launch canary; the resumed private receivers and settlement are real. Installed payloads and vendor execution remain synthetic. A parent owns the fixture root and stops and confirms the scope absent before removing it, including when a child assertion fails (`public_resumed_launch_uses_real_work_scope`). The five public refusal cases inject OS errors only at the actual inspector, helper pin, authority thread and socket boundaries, or install a real stale socket. Each runs over reply-settled and failed histories, requires the original reservation bytes, checks an exact run inventory (allowing only refreshed derived environment bytes and the attempt's nonce-named refusal evidence), observes an empty work cgroup, and retries the identical launch successfully. A restored refusal creates no failed round or thread choice; a genuine failed history retains its choice. The custom harness prints the executable case names and timings; its functions are not test-profile label mints. Three retained-attempt controls keep a real process alive in the work cgroup, replace one request byte without changing its custody or length, and inject an error after the real intent publication has synced. They require the preparing record and durable refusal to remain, then the fixture parent stops the entire scope before removing its own root (`public_resumed_rollback_retains_live_work_process`; `public_resumed_rollback_retains_changed_request`; `public_resumed_rollback_retains_dispatch_intent`). These are live refusal controls, not crash recovery or retirement. The inspector case places `alpha/R` through the owner placement writer beneath its own fixture root, then checks exact restoration and both plan members after the identical retry settles. The driver installs a runs-directory replacement watch before reading its reservation, then waits for events until its PID and consumed intent are published; a 180-second bound only detects a hung fixture. Scope, cleanup and driver-wait timings are printed for the test's hang-guard margins (`public_resumed_inspector_refusal_restores_and_retries`).

Before a resumed launch publishes its reservation, mail verification uses the read-only receiver even for apply. An apply whose retained mail no longer matches its delivery therefore refuses as its preview does, instead of rewriting the file; the identical `bind-mail` restores changed bytes from the journal. The received-custody helper preflight also reads the saved client environment and prepares the round request in memory, checking the exact final name and its own exact temporary name for occupation. Inspection repeats that preparation before replacing `launch/runtime.env`; a missing binding or occupied request therefore cannot replace the environment first. Generic pre-command reconciliation already excludes every `launch`; a built-command regression pins this existing protection for a refused resumed invocation, whose own interrupted-round path validates selected recovery. The built private-entry fixture compares the whole runs tree, including file bytes and identities, for missing binding, invalid environment, foreign environment owner, occupied request and wrong state. It also invokes the built `_inspect-round --record` receiver with received custody and a recorded synthetic prior thread, requiring missing binding and occupied request refusals without replacing the environment or adding a temporary. Failed request publication removes its own created temporary, preserving an already occupied temporary and the final destination (``test:unit:round-request-failure-removes-only-its-temporary``). Lock acquisition remains synchronization; the live rollback below covers refusals before dispatch intent, while crashes and post-intent failures still require separate recovery (``test:unit:round-inspection-validates-before-publication``; ``test:integration:resumed-launch-validates-before-reconciliation``; ``test:crate:built-helper-resume-retains-mail-custody``).

A resumed apply embeds the exact settled reservation bytes, their length and a sixteen-hex SHA-256 consistency handle, an independently random attempt nonce and the expected request bytes in the preparing reservation. The handle follows the default one-million-attempt collision budget and supplies no authentication; the protected owner record supplies custody (``calc:digests:default-handle-size``). The complete record must still fit the 4 MiB reader bound before publication; embedding the raw bytes means a settled record above about half that bound can refuse with `round-rollback: preparing reservation is N bytes; limit is 4194304 bytes`. Both the raw reservation read and the embedded before-image use the placement-aware reader, preserving legacy decoding when placement is absent and refusing duplicate keys on the placement path (``test:unit:live-round-raw-reader-preserves-placement-floor``; ``test:unit:live-round-before-image-preserves-placement-floor``; ``test:unit:live-round-parent-publication-takes-mailbox-lock``). On a live refusal before dispatch intent, the launcher requires its inspector and helper process groups gone, the recorded work subtree empty or collected, and the container inventory empty. A private cleanup under received custody checks request bytes and owner/mode, exact nonce temporary names and socket identities before removing the attempt's files. The original reservation is restored last through the held runs descriptor and the original refusal ends with `rollback: restored`; uncertain absence, changed custody or a surviving temporary instead appends `retained:` and its reason. Attempt evidence is written through the held runs root before the first absence or before-image check, including when either check retains the attempt. If writing that evidence itself fails, the refusal reports the storage error and retains the attempt. For `retained:`, leave the run and its files alone. The helper-parent callback takes the same mailbox lock as the helper identity publisher. Attempt evidence remains outside the reservation at the protected runs root; retirement does not yet inventory these refusal files. Derived environment and credential refreshes are never reversed. The driver spawn is fenced by a persisted intent; after its PID/start receipt, the before-image and intent are discarded. A crash before intent leaves the before-image but no automatic restoration: later launch or settlement may refuse missing completions and ordinary reconciliation can mark the record failed. Existing stranded runs and crash recovery remain separate work; the five-refusal live host acceptance is described above. Focused tests pin exact restoration, process absence, replaced request preservation, the dispatch boundary and the whole-record bound; the built two-uid private cleanup refuses changed request content and replaced sockets, admits its exact request, and leaves reservation restoration to the host (``test:unit:live-round-rollback-restores-exact-bytes-last``; ``test:unit:live-round-rollback-waits-for-process-absence``; ``test:unit:live-round-rollback-checks-scope-and-container-absence``; ``test:unit:live-round-rollback-retains-replaced-request``; ``test:unit:live-round-rollback-refuses-after-dispatch-intent``; ``test:unit:live-round-before-image-keeps-reservation-bound``; ``test:unit:live-round-reservation-failure-removes-own-temporary``; ``test:unit:live-round-restoration-keeps-the-held-root``; ``test:crate:built-round-rollback-cleanup-retains-custody``). A production-published workspace grant and placement are retained through preparation and exact restoration (``test:unit:live-round-rollback-restores-placed-reservation``).

The `bind-mail` field `resume_authorized:false` is a fixed statement that binding does not itself authorize a resume. It is not a result of the helper’s pin checks; the launch preview performs those checks. The field’s shape is unchanged.

**Protocol (Reply evidence)** · `proto:superlooplane:reply-mail-binding` A worker sends its reply through its outbox to its loop. `bind-reply LOOP RUN --step STEP --delivery UTC_INSTANT` verifies the inverse worker-to-loop delivery and retains its opaque bytes for settlement; it grants no steering or resume authority. Settlement compares the retained reply with the protected delivery binding, including the selected worker identity and digest. Both binding forms hold the reservation mailbox lock. Retirement releases the worker's service pin family only after custody transfer and configuration retirement. Journal compaction and pin publication share an admission lock; pruning re-reads the pin projection while holding it.

**Protocol (Seeded source handoff)** · `proto:superlooplane:seeded-source-handoff` `Sources: NAME [NAME...]` or `Sources: NAME@HEX [NAME@HEX...]` in the opening declarations names at most sixteen local Git bares, with HEX a full forty-character commit object ID; an unseeded bare is empty, while a seeded bare contains the named commit's reachable objects and only `refs/seeds/NAME`, fetched from the loop's private `src/NAME.git` before worker ownership, has `HEAD` pointing to the unborn `refs/heads/lane/RUN` branch, and the reservation records the seed, source identity and instant. For a seeded bare the worker runs `git clone "$HOME/.local/sources/NAME.git" NAME`, `git -C NAME fetch origin refs/seeds/NAME:refs/seeds/NAME`, then `git -C NAME switch -c "lane/RUN" refs/seeds/NAME`, and ships its committed `refs/heads/lane/RUN` with `git push "$HOME/.local/sources/NAME.git" refs/heads/lane/RUN:refs/heads/lane/RUN`; an absent or empty declaration creates none, and a worker-created bare is recorded as undeclared at checkpoint. Source movement follows (`[TOOLS-rule:buildcontracts:repository-boundaries]`).

**Protocol (Checkpoint preservation)** · `proto:superlooplane:checkpoint-preservation` `superloop-lane checkpoint LOOP RUN --dry-run` lists the settled run's retained legacy home slots with sizes and each bare's lane-ref state without writing a checkpoint. The changing form copies the inert legacy `.local/inbox` and `.local/outbox` slots and every `.local/sources` bare through held directory descriptors into the next `preserved/N` under the run's driver directory, makes the copy loop-owned, checks each copied file's size and SHA-256 digest, and checks each copied bare's ref against the source, with Git connectivity checks when a lane object exists. The owner-protected reservation records the checkpoint index, instant, settled round, file sizes and digests, and for each bare `lane_ref: absent` with a null object before the worker ships to it or `lane_ref: present` with its verified object after a shipped round; a later absence of a previously recorded ref refuses by name. Harvest skips an unborn bare when another bare shipped and refuses a checkpoint with no shipped bare. An incomplete copy remains in place with a refusal attempt recorded and no complete checkpoint claim. Source directories may carry setgid, which changes group inheritance without relaxing the checked ownership, owner access, or prohibition on group/other writes; exact pinned modes still apply apart from that bit. This is the lane's checkpoint implementation of (`[TOOLS-rule:workercontracts:letters-and-retention]`).

**Procedure (External checkpoint checkout)** · `alg:superlooplane:external-checkpoint-checkout` `superloop-lane checkout LOOP RUN --repo REPO --into DIR [--dry-run]` prepares an external clean checkout from the latest complete checkpoint's preserved `sources/REPO.git`. It refuses a missing or changed preservation, a nonempty or linked destination, and a destination beneath the owner's temporary home root or the runs root. The whole temporary home root is reserved for run homes, so an external checkout belongs elsewhere. Git transfers a pack between children bound to held source and destination directory descriptors, checks out `refs/heads/lane/RUN` as a local branch, sets the lane identity and disables signing, then verifies the object, clean status and directory inode. A token-bound pending record precedes creation; failure leaves the directory for the owner and records an attempt, including recovery after a private timeout. Successful checkouts are retained by source and checkpoint in a bounded list with an omission count. The dry run previews the source object and destination without writing.

**Procedure (Focused library checkout proof)** · `alg:superlooplane:focused-library-checkout-proof` Build the separate executable before a focused library test that launches it: `cargo build --locked -p superloop-lane --bin superloop-lane && cargo test --locked -p superloop-lane --lib FILTER -- --exact`. Use the same Cargo profile for both commands. A focused `cargo test --lib` alone does not rebuild the executable, so the fixture refuses an executable older than any source in the record Cargo writes beside it, `superloop-lane.d`, or one whose record is missing, and names this build command.

**Procedure (On-demand contention-credit proof)** · `alg:superlooplane:on-demand-contention-credit-proof` Run `cargo test --locked -p superloop-lane --lib door::tests::door_two_requesters_credit_contention_before_unlocked_triage -- --ignored --exact` when validating real service behavior. This one end-to-end test keeps a six-second real hold to prove that credited store contention preserves an agent's three-second budget; the door's remaining-time calculation has no controlled clock, so ordinary and Meson slow gates exclude it. The store's lock credit, deadline and progress thresholds have separate controlled-clock tests in the default battery.

**Rule (Import evidence retention)** · `rule:superlooplane:import-evidence-retention` `retention = "keep"` in `workers.toml` is the only supported retention policy and is the default when absent. `superloop-lane mark-imported LOOP RUN --object HEX` verifies the preserved checkpoint copy before recording which checkpoint and bare explain the import. A marked object's checkout tuple remains in the bounded checkout list and never contributes to `checkouts_omitted`; `retire` refuses `retire-import-evidence` while such a mark exists. The retention authority is (`[TOOLS-rule:workercontracts:letters-and-retention]`).

**Protocol (Harvest gate agreement)** · `proto:superlooplane:harvest-gate-agreement` `superloop-lane harvest LOOP RUN --gate-object HEX [--dry-run]` compares the latest complete checkpoint's preserved lane object with the server-reported checked-out object from the successful external gate, records their agreement under the mailbox lock, then checks every current inbox, outbox and source-bare file against the preserved sizes and digests before collecting the disposable home. The dry run previews the comparison and planned removals without writing. A mismatch, missing checkpoint, changed file or added source bare leaves the home; collection unlinks only beneath pinned home descriptors and records complete or interrupted disposition with observed remaining components. The run record and preservation remain under the runs root, and collection does not retire the run or release its worker UID. Collection applies (`[TOOLS-rule:workercontracts:letters-and-retention]`) through the recorded gate object and checkpoint.

**Precondition (Launch input checks)** · `pre:superlooplane:launch-input-checks` Launch checks mount roots, installed input hashes, the backend grant and local image identifier before container start, then saves its checks in the durable driver directory; the `make-home` stdout report and saved plan describe creation. Worker settings supply memory, process count and CPU shares; the registry supplies loop identity and runs root.

The protected backend selects the installed program, configuration and charter hashes and its vendor template directory. In a mixed installation, Claude uses the sibling ``runner-templates/claude/<image-hash>`` directory even though the shared ``template_dir`` setting names the Codex primary. The build report and installation generation must agree with that vendor's manifest; no other vendor's program or hash is a fallback. Claude's runtime and credential acceptance remain unavailable.

**Warning (Runtime environment trust)** · `warn:superlooplane:runtime-environment-trust` `runtime.env` is loop-group readable in a loop-owned run directory. Its recorded root identity lets an honest client reject a replaced runs root; the loop identity can replace this file, so it is not a boundary against that identity.

**Invariant (Run authority separation)** · `inv:superlooplane:run-authority-separation` The host session owner can reach the rootless image store, registry and loop settings; its namespace-owner helper checks one protected run reservation, accepts container requests from descendants of that run's launched driver session, and permits another attempt only after a bounded model-capacity error from the prior nonzero attempt. The host pins the entry, Podman and hashing executables through protected, link-free components before namespace entry; the helper rechecks the held Podman file and executes it by descriptor for attempts, stop, wait, listing, inspection and removal, so replacing a pathname cannot retarget its program. The loop-UID driver can write its durable driver directory but cannot change the owner-controlled worker-home root, installed runtime template, identity bind files, container identifier files, authoritative observation or helper sockets; the worker-owned home partitions are not writable by the driver. The worker uses its allocated UID inside the box and can write its planned writable mounts and runtime tmpfs; the owner-only sign-in directory is never mounted into a run. Session ancestry uses the recorded PID and process start tick without a pidfd, so a reuse of both values is not formally excluded. The worker and driver roles are defined by (`[TOOLS-rule:workercontracts:launch-and-evidence]`) and (`[TOOLS-rule:workercontracts:launch-and-evidence]`); external evidence is governed by (`[TOOLS-rule:workercontracts:launch-and-evidence]`).

## Commands · `sec:readme:commands`

**Definition (Request helper boundary)** · `def:superlooplane:request-helper-boundary` The five request commands `requests status|list|triage|review|adjudicate LOOP` call the separately released private `requests-helper` at its configured absolute path. Native builds set that path to `libexec/superloop/requests-helper` under the install prefix; `SUPERLOOP_LANE_REQUESTS_HELPER` selects an explicit replacement, without sibling-name or PATH lookup. `PERMISSION_TRIAGE_LOOP_REGISTRY` names the existing private loop registry. Permission judgements additionally require the helper's validated `PERMISSION_TRIAGE_REQUEST_CONTEXT`; the lane never grants permission authority itself.

**Procedure (Permission registry provisioning)** · `alg:superlooplane:permission-registry-provisioning` `requests provision-registry LOOP [--state-root DIR] [--dry-run]` derives the private permission entry from the protected estate registry row's name, session, launch directory, UID and loop home. It requires a registered session and refuses unsafe sources or destinations as `requests-registry-refusal`. The destination is `PERMISSION_TRIAGE_LOOP_REGISTRY`, or `~/.local/state/superloop/requests-registry` for provisioning; export that path for request readers and the door service. Permission state defaults to `~/.local/state/superloop/permission-state/LOOP`; select an existing journal with `--state-root DIR`. Both destinations must be normal paths beneath the calling home, separate from the loop home and authorization sources. The preview creates nothing. Apply creates private directories and an atomic mode-0600 entry, saves a `LOOP.provision.json` deploy report beside it, and leaves an identical entry unchanged on repetition. Existing nonprivate directories, linked paths and nonprivate entries refuse; the command does not repair their permissions or migrate journals. Deploy the private helper and its build-time binding before provisioning and verify `requests status LOOP` afterwards. The report also requires `direct_snapshot` and `direct_journal`, naming the registered policy file and separate direct journal. Apply republishes policy; the journal is never opened by provisioning. Run loop-home ensure for both tiers before provisioning. The deploy operator and the supervising loop read `LOOP.provision.json` as deployment evidence; `requests-helper` skips this non-`.env` file, although it counts toward the registry listing limits of 1,024 names and 65,536 bytes. The `changed` field excludes the report, which every successful apply rewrites, `changed=false` included. Reports include the pinned registry and state directory identities as `registry_identity` and `state_identity` (`st_dev`, `st_ino`), or null for an absent preview destination; `created_registry` and `created_state` describe actual creation, while `would_create_registry` and `would_create_state` describe absent preview destinations. Provisioning refuses destinations inside any estate loop home and state roots overlapping another private entry, using the helper’s deployment rules, which `superloop-lane` invokes through the private helper on its descriptor-pinned snapshot; deploy it and `requests-helper` from the same commit so both judge the registry by the same rules. A different existing `LOOP_STATE_ROOT` refuses by name; retain it with `--state-root` rather than silently selecting the default.

**Rule (Door peer capacity)** · `rule:superlooplane:door-peer-capacity` Each door service process has a fixed limit of sixteen connected peers, set in the service implementation. This is a connection resource bound before peer authentication and request classification, not a count of node compute slots and not a per-loop node registration setting. Git, compute and requests waiting on authenticated admission all consume it. The service does not queue unauthenticated excess peers: it immediately sends status 125 with `door-capacity-refusal`, `peer_limit: 16`, `retry_after_seconds: 120` and `queue_state: no-queue`, without dispatching a command or creating a request record. Releasing a peer admits a later connection; no FIFO or reservation is promised. The stub prints the peer limit and retry cadence. A worker treats this as transient and may repeat the same request after at least 120 seconds within its existing step budget; it does not extend that budget, increase lane concurrency or alter registration. Node scheduling remains the owner-controlled wrapper's responsibility. Raising the service peer bound requires a reviewed service change and deploy, not a worker argument or a larger lane count.

**Rule (Door request admission)** · `rule:superlooplane:door-request-admission` The door admits one compute request and one Git receive request concurrently for an authenticated box across service and exercise processes; a second request of either class waits, and every other operation retains exclusive box admission. Only operations validated against the registered route select the two concurrent classes. The shared per-loop, per-worker-UID guard excludes other operations, and separate exclusive compute and receive guards exclude same-class overlap. A receive changes the registered mirror while compute uses its own checkout; the receive still undergoes ordinary judgement and registered-ref validation. The door holds every admission guard through the effect, its durable record and the final reply. In allow mode, admission ends when that final reply has been sent. The agent’s record and the waiting publication may finish afterwards, and a later request of the same worker may be admitted and filed before they do, as another worker’s request already can. Such a request sees the shape’s judgement history as recorded so far, which may not yet carry the earlier agent’s verdict. Its learned decision and the class’s effective mode are unaffected, because only an owner label creates a learned decision and only an adjudicated enforce-mode record with an opposing owner label makes a correction pending. The waiting publication receives a fresh 300-second lock-admission allowance, extended to the original request deadline plus its full possible wait credit when that is later; this bounds lock admission, not the helper’s work. It emits no socket credit. Exhaustion leaves the recorded effect available for recovery. A post-reply failure is recorded in the service journal and listed in request status, and no frame follows the final reply. The door polls a held guard every 100 milliseconds before creating any record, holding only the existing peer slot while waiting. The admission wait is capped at the smaller of 30 seconds and the request’s remaining deadline less a two-second reply margin; no remaining margin means immediate refusal on contention. A guard released within that wait admits the request normally. An exhausted wait refuses with reason `door-request-concurrent` and status 125, adds `waited_seconds` as the actual wait rounded to a millisecond and integer `retry_after_seconds: 30`, and retains `admission_window` as one sentence explaining the wait and retry advice. The worker’s stderr line keeps the reason prefix and prints `waited=` and `retry_after=` figures. Two boxes of one loop retain independent admission. Store locks cover custody transactions, including index maintenance and syncs, and are released before command execution or agent judgement. The actual-package acceptance uses the production composer, receiver and real namespace worker entry with a completed delivery. A host-root seam roots the walk at the fixture home and its owner in place of the normal root-owned ancestor walk, so the case does not depend on the owner or mode of the temporary directory; the prefix tests cover that walk independently. The normal command still selects the filesystem root, root uid and its current executable, with no new command-line or environment override. This case does not re-execute the top-level public argument dispatch or its preceding reaping reconciliation; the public override cases run both up to the override refusal. It repeats the binding, requires identical reservation bytes and checks the published evidence bytes. Mutations of the vector producer and reservation publication each fail this case. Override cases for both public binding commands supply only reconciliation settings, not a complete plan, and require override refusal before plan loading. ## Claude credential acceptance draft · `sec:superlooplane:claude-credential-acceptance-draft`

This is a draft handoff for the loop's review and owner-operated trial, not an executable procedure or a record of vendor acceptance. The production runtime gate remains closed. Publication of the reviewed program on the compute node waits on the trial loop's accepted build report of the runbook's reviewed box inputs rather than on a loop step; that report supplies the program path, version, digest and source, and an unrelated executable copied onto the compute node cannot supply them. After it, the loop names the isolated trial, the operators and the reviewed observation method before any credential-bearing run, and the trial's cases below establish token-only startup, no worker refresh and reload after rejection with that program before the adapter is relied on. A worker prepares code and synthetic fixtures without accessing a live sign-in. The sign-in stays in the owner helper's home; the worker receives the six-member projection described by (`proto:superlooplane:claude-owner-credential-encoding`). The trial never revokes an owner's shared login or edits source expiry to manufacture a result.

The trial records the public program version and digest, matching installation reference, candidate source revision and reviewed method revision. Each case has a separate outcome: measured pass, measured refusal or unmeasured with the missing observation named. A pass in one case cannot supply another case's evidence. The helper namespace acceptance first establishes the production custody, configuration-anchor and publication routes with the installed pinned executable from that report, distinct from the vendor cases; the existing mapped-identity case and the held-program stand-in case (`sec:superlooplane:claude-held-program-standin`) exercise those routes before that program exists and do not establish that result.

|Case|Evidence required for a measured pass|Insufficient evidence|
|----|-------------------------------------|---------------------|
|External token-only startup|The pinned program performs a bounded authenticated operation from the worker-owned home using the helper's published projection, with no owner-home bind, refresh fields, plugin state or inherited alternative credential source.|A matching file, a successful owner-home print probe or a stand-in's output.|
|No worker refresh authority or attempt|The reviewed observer distinguishes worker activity from owner-helper activity and identifies refresh attempts throughout the stated interval, including the rejection case. It establishes both absent refresh authority in the worker and no worker refresh attempt in that interval, and records whether the worker file changed outside a helper publication. A changed file is a signal to investigate, not proof of a refresh attempt by itself.|No refresh field alone; no successful refresh; a tunnel's host-level connection counts when authentication and refresh share that host.|
|Reload after rejection|One round's same live ``claude -p`` process first uses the old authority, encounters a credential rejection, then reads the newly published projection and succeeds, without a process restart, new login or owner-source bind. The observer orders publication, rejection, read and recovery, checks the on-disk projection before the read, and compares PID together with process start time, reporting unchanged identity as a boolean.|A fresh process succeeding, a resumed process with a new identity, an unchanged-file comparison or a process that reloads before any rejection.|

Across all three cases, the owner-operated observer compares worker-file presence, identity and bytes with the last observed helper publication, recording only comparison booleans. It records whether a change occurred outside a helper publication, including clearing, removal or replacement; no credential bytes, lengths or digests leave the observer. Ordered publication evidence is required to distinguish a helper replacement from another writer. A missed interval or ambiguous attribution is unmeasured, not an unchanged-file result. File stability alone cannot rule out a refresh attempt that leaves no file change.

The loop must review how the pinned program is kept alive across those operations and how rejection and the subsequent credential read are distinguished without recording credential bytes. Republishing up to 600 seconds ahead does not itself reject the old in-memory token: a natural rejection can occur only at or after that token's expiry, or if rotation revokes it. The same round's print-mode process must span that boundary. Natural expiry and owner-helper republication are a candidate sequence, not an assumed vendor behavior: the program may reload before rejection, exit on rejection or hide the necessary observation. Such a run records its actual outcome and leaves reload-on-rejection unmeasured. No unsupported interactive command, response fixture or refresh endpoint is invented to fill that gap. A copied token elsewhere in the collectable worker home requires a separate inspection; the publication comparison alone cannot exclude it (`proto:superlooplane:claude-credential-exercise-payload`).

The loop's refresh-margin observation uses natural expiry in the helper's own home, the held program, its production print vector and timeout, with vendor output discarded. It checks the lock path against the production Claude lock before operating. An isolated observation quiesces competing helpers and owner CLI writers; the lock does not constrain an independent owner login. Separate natural cycles sample above and at 600 seconds remaining, then smaller intervals if authority stays unchanged; a rotation starts a new cycle. Source changes during the observation are recorded, not overwritten or attributed to the probe without evidence. The owner program alone refreshes and writes the source.

For refresh margin, record remaining milliseconds and window membership before and after each bounded print call, token-changed and file-identity-changed booleans, exit or timeout and call duration. Distinguish positive unrotated authority, rotation and expired refusal. A successful print exit alone does not establish a guaranteed 600-second lifetime.

For program binding cost, time the first production binding, unchanged dispatcher checks and the check immediately before print separately within the actual held-custody activation and thread. Record cold or warm storage conditions and activation boundaries. The independent script's hash wall and the synthetic benchmark do not measure the pinned program's production cost.

For periodic contention, follow the isolated baseline with two identified trial helpers and observe their lock waits, pass durations, call counts and publication outcomes through the same natural window, identifying preparation and round publication separately. The pre-expiry refresh-ahead interval is 600 seconds: at most twenty periodic calls per helper fit that interval, with the sleep after each pass and work or contention reducing the count. This is not a lifetime bound. Observe the expired, unrotated regime separately over a declared bounded interval: each subsequent pass can call print again and refuse publication. Window membership alone cannot separate that regime, since the refresh-ahead test also holds past expiry, so its passes are those with remaining milliseconds at or below zero; report their call, pass and publication counts, lock-wait and pass durations and refusal categories under a case identifier of their own, apart from the pre-expiry window, and record that case as unmeasured if it is not observed. Preparation and round-publication calls are counted separately from periodic passes.

For publication and refusal, observe both live Claude recipients through the production dispatcher and exercise reader. The publisher compares the newly derived projection with its own last-published bytes, not the current worker file. An unchanged cache entry suppresses writes but not due print calls; a worker rewrite, clearing or removal is not repaired merely by another pass. The observer therefore checks the on-disk file against the last helper publication before attributing a reload result. Expired authority or failed refresh causes no helper replacement; this does not assert that another writer left the prior file intact. This draft selects no repair policy. Schema-2 provider identity alone does not establish token rotation, and a synthetic expiry test does not establish vendor acceptance.

The evidence output is an allow-list for a future reviewed observer, not a new implemented report schema: public program identity and candidate source revision, case and method identifiers, outcome, exit status, observation times, durations, remaining milliseconds, window membership, call, pass and publication counts, comparison booleans and fixed storage-condition and refusal categories. Token and file-identity comparisons occur only inside the owner-operated observer; no credential value, length, prefix, digest, source JSON, vendor output or arbitrary exception text enters the report. **Protocol (Workspace declaration through launch)** · `proto:superlooplane:workspace-launch-declaration`

An owner opts a new run into a compute workspace with `launch LOOP RUN --letter FILE --compute-workspace JSON --dry-run`. The grant names the loop, run, node identity, absolute workspace, ordered baseline environment including PATH, named resources, shells, quick-policy digest, blacklist digest and executor protocol. The declaration is at most 65,536 encoded bytes. This stage does not contact the node or create, inspect, clone, clean or execute in the workspace; its path and contents remain the superloop's responsibility.

The loop publishes `workspace_policy = { quick_policy_digest = "HEX", blacklist_digest = "HEX" }` in its existing ``workers.toml``. Both values are lowercase 64-digit SHA-256 identities and both must match the declaration. The table has exactly those two keys; it names policy identities, not per-run workspace state. This stage does not validate the policy bodies or interpret the node identity as proof of connectivity. The later door caller validates the authenticated node and the published policy bodies before classification.

Preview includes `plan.compute_workspace = {grant, digest}` and changes neither the reservation nor the worker allocation. Apply repeats the same declaration with `--expect-digest HEX`, using `workspace_declaration_digest` from the preview. For a grant-only declaration this equals the snapshot's digest; with a placement it binds both members as specified below. The digest binds the whole grant, including the loop, run, workspace, environment and both policy identities; it is not a digest of unrelated launch settings. A missing or changed digest refuses as `workspace-digest` before preparation. Private preparation receives the same declaration and digest and rechecks them against its held loop configuration before writing the protected reservation. For both declared and undeclared runs, mail birth is reserved before publishing the preparing reservation, followed by home construction and worker entry. Unknown fields, missing acknowledgement, malformed grants, foreign loop/run or policy identity, duplicate baseline names and recognizable credentials refuse as `workspace-declaration`, without echoing environment values.

A fresh launch reads and validates any existing reservation before probing the entry program or checking the box and proxy; a malformed reservation therefore reports its `uid-table` reservation read refusal first. Resource names in a declaration must be unique; duplicate JSON object keys refuse rather than selecting the last value.

Before birth reservation, a cut leaves nothing and launch can start afresh. A cut inside birth reservation can leave a partial receipt or binding without a run reservation, as for an undeclared launch. After birth reservation but before publication, the pending receipt and binding exist but retirement has no reservation naming them; a same-run relaunch selects another uid because the pending uid is reserved, then refuses at the receipt whose identity differs; use a new run name, or have the owner reconcile the pending birth. After publication, including during home construction and birth completion, the reservation names the reserved birth and retirement can retire it without relaunching; a retry without a declaration reuses that identity and reserves it idempotently.

The baseline environment is public configuration: it must contain no secret because it appears in previews, protected plans, worker context and agent input. Declaration rejects names containing TOKEN, SECRET, PASSWORD, PASSWD, CREDENTIAL, `API_KEY`, `PRIVATE_KEY`, `ACCESS_KEY`, AUTH or COOKIE, and values recognizable as private-key PEM, bearer credentials, common vendor token prefixes or credential-bearing URLs. This is a limited recognition check, not a secret detector; the explicit owner acknowledgement covers values the check cannot recognize. Supply credentials through the separately admitted credential route, never the baseline. Environment names are case-sensitive for uniqueness. **Protocol (Own-path placement reader)** · `proto:superlooplane:workspace-placement-reader`

A run's protected plan may carry `compute_placement`, the owner's record of an own-path workspace root, written once with the grant and retained at every plan rebuild. It names the placement, the loop, run and node, the grant's digest, the root path, which equals the grant's workspace, and the root's identity: the filesystem UUID with the root directory's file handle type and bytes. Before execution, the caller compares that identity with the one read from the opened root, refusing as `door-workspace-context-differs` on any difference. The identity survives reboots and array renumbering; it does not survive a recreated, restored or copied root, which needs a new placement. Unknown fields, duplicate keys, a mode other than `own-path`, or a record whose loop, run, node, digest or root differs from the grant refuse when the plan is read.

The member has exactly schema (1), placement (32 lowercase hexadecimal digits), loop, run, node, `grant_digest`, mode (own-path), root, `root_identity` and seed. Root identity has exactly `filesystem_uuid` (32 lowercase hexadecimal digits), `handle_type` (a signed 32-bit integer) and handle (lowercase hexadecimal encoding of 1 to 128 bytes). Seed is required and is null or a shape-checked publisher generation identifier: one to 64 ASCII letters, digits, dot, underscore or hyphen, starting with a letter or digit. It supplies no authority. The full grant digest binds these owner-held facts to the protected workspace declaration rather than to a requester's substitute grant; the placement identifier names a census entry and is not authority. There is no cleanup list. Namespace identity, holder identity and seed metadata beyond its identifier are deferred and refused as unknown fields.

Byte readers reject duplicates along the path to placement: the enclosing plan, `compute_placement` and every nested placement key, including an earlier plan hidden by a later duplicate. Rewrite journals apply the same check to their embedded before and after reservations. Duplicates elsewhere retain the existing JSON decoding behavior. Reservations without placement keep their existing decoded value and syntax refusal text. A caller with plan bytes uses the placement-aware byte reader before converting them to a generic value.

The descriptor reader obtains the file handle with an empty-path `name_to_handle_at` call and the UUID with `FS_IOC_GETFSUUID`, falling back only when that interface is unsupported to the trusted device-UUID links matching the opened directory's device. An unreadable identity fails closed. The owner commands below use the same library reader on the node; the unselected executor requires the placement and binds the descriptor it opened before opening cwd or spawning. Install these readers everywhere reservations are consumed before the owner publishes `compute_placement`; an older reader can initially preserve the unknown JSON member but loses it on reconstruction and refuses the protected-plan comparison. Existing runs require no migration. Neither legacy node payload changes; the owner commands do require the matching lane binary installed on the node.

The placement, executor and native-creation acceptance tests require their temporary roots to provide directory handles through `name_to_handle_at` and a filesystem UUID through the reader's supported routes. The gate slot (`infra/gate/run-slot.sh`) sets each round's `TMPDIR` beneath its slot root, so that root must lie on such a filesystem, and a direct test run needs the same of its own `TMPDIR` (`[TOOLS-req:deployment:verification-programs]`); a missing interface is an unmet gate prerequisite, never a skipped custody proof. The live identity test fails with that prerequisite named, consistently with the dependent command, executor and publication fixtures.

The explicit parent is absolute, owned by the entered owner and mode 0700, opened without following any symlink. The grant's W must be the run name directly beneath it, with `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`, `CARGO_HOME` and `TMPDIR` in its public baseline naming its target, cargo and tmp children. Every existing destination refuses, including an owner-owned directory or link; grant paths outside this placer keep their general contract. Preview reads the parent identity and checks absence, creating nothing. The node program reads UUID and file handle through the same lane-library function as the executor; no second identity implementation or automatic SSH transport exists. After exclusive creation, any error leaves the named root for the census and performs no automatic cleanup.

A directory created before a crash stays discoverable even when no receipt came back: the dedicated parent's immediate entries are the inventory, so no second durable placement inventory needs a crash contract. `placement inventory --parent DIR` validates and enumerates that parent only, never descending into a workspace or following an entry link. Valid bindings, unreferenced directory candidates, replaced roots, unknown entries and unreadable reservations remain distinct. A `live-binding` means a matching non-retired reservation, not proof that a process is running. The result is point-in-time evidence, not removal authority. Each entry costs at most one reservation read; there is no scan of unrelated runs, node directories or workspace contents. **Protocol (Unselected workspace node executor)** · `proto:superlooplane:workspace-node-executor`

The node executor is a library entry with no installed caller or workspace route. Its custody-owning caller supplies a trusted reservation, the authenticated node identity and the exact workspace envelope. It reads the grant only through the protected plan's workspace snapshot reader, never settings, and checks the enclosing loop, run, node and grant identity before opening the existing absolute workspace and relative cwd. The walk refuses symlinks and retains the cwd descriptor through execution, so renaming the workspace does not redirect an already prepared command. Preparation provisions no directory, checkout, target or other workspace content.

The caller must authorize that exact envelope before execution: a quick rule binds the program's PATH resolution and every environment addition; a miss, including every shell form, waits for judgement in every mode. The executor is not an admission rule and cannot make a quick allow or deny. It clears inherited environment, installs the grant's public baseline and applies ordered additions, with the last assignment winning if a judged request repeats a name. Argv bytes are passed without shell interpretation; a shell runs only its grant-named program with `-c` and the unchanged text. Standard input is empty. Standard output and error stream to caller-owned descriptors without buffering or interpretation as measurements.

One monotonic deadline starts at preparation and covers every sequence step. The caller connects outer termination to a cancellation flag; setting it revokes the remaining budget and yields a timeout with the same stopped-prefix evidence. The library installs no process-global signal handler. Nonzero exit, signal, timeout or unknown outcome stops the sequence. Every attempted spawn retains a step, including a killed step or an unknown spawn/wait result; an empty timeout prefix means no spawn was attempted. Wall is measured at the node; CPU is the reaped child's user and system usage, not a process-wide delta or a command's report. Unknown CPU remains null. On completion or expiry the executor stops the child's process group before reaping its leader. This is command mediation under the node account, not confinement: a command can leave its process group, and filesystem effects remain those the account can perform.

The library returns existing measurement and outcome types without writing custody, adding a record member or serializing a new response. The caller will bind these measurements to the admitted envelope, grant and policy revisions in the existing schema-seven effect. The placer still owns content and warm-build correctness; W3 owns authentication, admission, transport selection and publication. The legacy compute route and both node payloads are unchanged. Installing this unselected implementation changes no running service behavior and requires no rewrite, restart or node action; before any later caller writes workspace effects, every custody reader must carry the workspace reader floor, and a preflight writer must additionally require the smaller-prefix floor (``proto:superlooplane:workspace-readers``).
