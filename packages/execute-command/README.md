# Execute command · `guide:executecommand:execute-command`

**Definition (Status and ownership)** · `def:executecommand:package-status` This internal tier 1 library provides the shared vocabulary, persistence boundary and pure checks and policy functions of (`[TOOLS-spec:executecommand:process]`) used by the door, permission hook, gateway and public superloop command entry. PERMISSIONTRIAGE is the accountable domain owner named by (`[TOOLS-dec:executecommand:status]`); EXECUTECOMMAND is the linter's derived corpus owner for this package's labels. The callers supply authenticated observations and render shared decisions into their existing record and wire formats.

**Rule (Independent domain)** · `rule:executecommand:domain-boundary` The library has no internal source dependencies and inherits only the workspace's admitted serde and serde_json dependencies. It neither imports request-core nor discovers identity, custody, configuration, execution or transport from ambient state; callers implement explicit adapters under (`[TOOLS-dec:executecommand:request-core]`) and (`[TOOLS-dec:executecommand:adapters]`). The module responsibilities follow (`[TOOLS-tab:executecommand:modules]`).

**Rule (Strict vocabulary)** · `rule:executecommand:strict-vocabulary` Schema-one requests bind exact command bytes or argv to caller-validated context. Context lane and run keys must be present, with explicit null when inapplicable. Readers reject unknown versions, fields, variants and malformed bounded values as execute-command-request-shape. Verdicts distinguish allow, refuse and refer from caller classifications and lifecycle states; both remedy fields must be present, with explicit null when inapplicable. A verdict's `tier` names its deciding rung, one of the six rungs of (`[TOOLS-tab:executecommand:ladder]`) from deterministic checks to the owner, and a referral's escalation names its target as loop review, operations or owner. A refusal without a correction or escalation, or a referral without escalation, refuses as execute-command-verdict-shape. Outcome records retain rationale separately from execution results; a published judgement is not execution evidence. Queue, judgement and execution walls are required-nullable milliseconds: null means unmeasured, while zero is a measurement. A measured execution wall on refused-before-effect or outcome-unknown refuses as execute-command-record-shape during deserialization or validated outcome construction. OutcomeRecord fields are private, so every outcome placed in a Snapshot has passed validation. OutcomeRecord deserialization refuses unknown or missing fields and invalid walls, and the first adapter that persists outcomes refuses a stored record of any other shape as execute-command-record-shape rather than reading it through a default. These rules implement (`[TOOLS-inv:executecommand:actionable-verdict]`) and (`[TOOLS-tab:executecommand:caller-vocabulary]`).

**Rule (Bounds)** · `rule:executecommand:bounds` Each nonblank text value is at most 4096 UTF-8 bytes; each evidence or argv list has at most 64 entries; exact command bytes are nonempty and at most 65536 bytes; a JSON envelope is at most 1048576 bytes. Bounds are interface limits, not a claim about policy or latency. Request and context digests are opaque caller-supplied identities derived from existing caller observations under (`[TOOLS-dec:executecommand:context-digest]`).

**Rule (Persistence contract)** · `rule:executecommand:persistence` The Persistence trait accepts load, conditional judgement commit, intent, claim and completion requests, with bounded fields and no arbitrary mutation closure. ConditionalCommit validates the verdict; Completion binds the outcome to the claim and revision. The adapter atomically checks identity, revision, custody, authority and transition preconditions; a refused operation changes nothing and strands neither intent nor claim. Only a durable result permits the next transition. CommitUncertain identifies the exact operation and requires Load::Resolve before retry or effect; a plain Load::Current naming the record with an unresolved publication returns CommitUncertain with that operation’s identity instead of a durable snapshot. A current read naming another binding is checked for conflict before uncertainty and never receives another record’s unresolved operation. Resolving a claim never grants a second execution attempt. The in-memory fault fixture proves refusal rollback, both published and unpublished uncertain writes, single-use claims and bound completion; real adapter durability and recovery are measured at their caller boundaries under (`[TOOLS-dec:executecommand:request-core]`).

**Rule (Pure checks)** · `rule:executecommand:checks` The checks module preserves the hook’s eight ordered hygiene predicates, shell grammar and disabled read claims, exact class/operation/shape matching for learned and registered rules, and the door’s correction-evidence refusals; caller-supplied observations determine scope validation, a miss grants no allowance, and every decided verdict retains both nullable remedy fields. Non-UTF-8 command bytes with a shell observation remain a miss: the permission-triage hook receives JSON strings and its classifier has no raw-byte rule to inherit. Copied caller inputs and expected outcomes pin parity without linking caller crates; harness decoding, audit silence, rendering and authenticated observation construction remain caller responsibilities.

**Rule (Pure policy)** · `rule:executecommand:policy` The policy module orders door modes as audit, enforce and allow, bounds effective mode by class, loop and registered floor, preserves named transition and evidence refusals, and offers floor-derived absent settings for caller publication under (`[TOOLS-dec:executecommand:initialization]`); floor, citation, transition and setting-evidence checks remain distinct so the caller preserves validation order and custody. Mode-transition allowances concern configuration publication only, and gateway admission preserves budget-before-snapshot-before-cooldown precedence with caller-supplied audit and clock observations, saturating arithmetic and actionable remedies; no policy function writes settings, executes commands or changes caller envelopes.

**Rule (Bounded judgement)** · `rule:executecommand:triage` The triage module validates the distinct DoorVerdict and HookSixClass profiles, includes cleanup in the bounded call budget and maps runner outcomes into common verdicts under (`[TOOLS-rule:executecommand:agent-failure]`). Invalid output, including a model scope below the observed floor, refers with its own status and never allows an effect. The rubric module supplies exact-byte digests and untrusted-data framing; protected loading and backend invocation remain with the adapters. Escalation answers bind request, context and current review after caller authentication. An unprovisioned external route carries execute-command-route-unprovisioned as its remedy; this is no claim of delivery or operations authority under (`[TOOLS-req:executecommand:escalation-acceptance]`). Existing caller envelopes retain their shapes.

The record boundary supplies review-state and current-review preconditions, completion retry classification, retained decision remedies and the hook audit publication exception. Learning classifies reviewed agreements, corrections and labels after undecided or referred predictions, and preserves an enforced correction’s audit override until a newer setting resolves it. These functions inspect caller-supplied evidence only; request-core remains the common transition engine, and custody owners retain locks and durable publication.

The process module orders reviewed proposal consumption through the ProposalCustody adapter: withdrawal, durable common claim, existing intent recovery or answer application, then completion. A failed claim stops before effect, a published execution intent is recovered without replay, and an interrupted completion retains the same outcome on retry. The adapter supplies authenticated observations and request-core transitions; the library neither opens a journal nor spawns a runner.

The snapshot module carries the owner-published policy observations of (`[TOOLS-dec:executecommand:direct-roots]`), their source revisions and canonical observation digests. It rejects unknown envelope shapes and changed observations as execute-command-record-shape. Filesystem custody, effective-UID authentication, bounded reads and publication under the owner store lock remain adapter responsibilities; deserializing a snapshot does not authenticate it.
