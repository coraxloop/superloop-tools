# Execute command · `guide:executecommand:execute-command`

**Definition (Status and ownership)** · `def:executecommand:package-status` This internal tier 1 library provides the shared vocabulary, persistence boundary and pure checks and policy functions of (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:domain-and-adapters]`) used by the door, permission hook, gateway and public superloop command entry. PERMISSIONTRIAGE is the accountable domain owner named by (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:domain-and-adapters]`); EXECUTECOMMAND is the linter's derived corpus owner for this package's labels. The callers supply authenticated observations and render shared decisions into their existing record and wire formats.

**Rule (Independent domain)** · `rule:executecommand:domain-boundary` The library has no internal source dependencies and inherits only the workspace's admitted serde and serde_json dependencies. It neither imports request-core nor discovers identity, custody, configuration, execution or transport from ambient state; callers implement explicit adapters under (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:domain-and-adapters]`) and (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:domain-and-adapters]`). The module responsibilities follow (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:domain-and-adapters]`).

**Rule (Strict vocabulary)** · `rule:executecommand:strict-vocabulary` Schema-one requests bind exact command bytes or argv to caller-validated context. Context lane and run keys must be present, with explicit null when inapplicable. Readers reject unknown versions, fields, variants and malformed bounded values as execute-command-request-shape. Verdicts distinguish allow, refuse and refer from caller classifications and lifecycle states; both remedy fields must be present, with explicit null when inapplicable. A verdict's `tier` names its deciding rung, one of the six rungs of (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:escalation]`) from deterministic checks to the owner, and a referral's escalation names its target as loop review, operations or owner. A refusal without a correction or escalation, or a referral without escalation, refuses as execute-command-verdict-shape. Outcome records retain rationale separately from execution results; a published judgement is not execution evidence. Queue, judgement and execution walls are required-nullable milliseconds: null means unmeasured, while zero is a measurement. A measured execution wall on refused-before-effect or outcome-unknown refuses as execute-command-record-shape during deserialization or validated outcome construction. OutcomeRecord fields are private, so every outcome placed in a Snapshot has passed validation. OutcomeRecord deserialization refuses unknown or missing fields and invalid walls, and the first adapter that persists outcomes refuses a stored record of any other shape as execute-command-record-shape rather than reading it through a default. These rules implement (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:escalation]`) and (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:escalation]`).

**Rule (Bounds)** · `rule:executecommand:bounds` Each nonblank text value is at most 4096 UTF-8 bytes; each evidence or argv list has at most 64 entries; exact command bytes are nonempty and at most 65536 bytes; a JSON envelope is at most 1048576 bytes. Bounds are interface limits, not a claim about policy or latency. Request and context digests are opaque caller-supplied identities derived from existing caller observations under (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:policy-and-binding]`).

**Rule (Persistence contract)** · `rule:executecommand:persistence` The Persistence trait accepts load, conditional judgement commit, intent, claim and completion requests, with bounded fields and no arbitrary mutation closure. ConditionalCommit validates the verdict; Completion binds the outcome to the claim and revision. The adapter atomically checks identity, revision, custody, authority and transition preconditions; a refused operation changes nothing and strands neither intent nor claim. Only a durable result permits the next transition. CommitUncertain identifies the exact operation and requires Load::Resolve before retry or effect; a plain Load::Current naming the record with an unresolved publication returns CommitUncertain with that operation’s identity instead of a durable snapshot. A current read naming another binding is checked for conflict before uncertainty and never receives another record’s unresolved operation. Resolving a claim never grants a second execution attempt. The in-memory fault fixture proves refusal rollback, both published and unpublished uncertain writes, single-use claims and bound completion; real adapter durability and recovery are measured at their caller boundaries under (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:domain-and-adapters]`).

**Rule (Pure checks)** · `rule:executecommand:checks` The checks module preserves the hook’s eight ordered hygiene predicates, shell grammar and disabled read claims, exact class/operation/shape matching for learned and registered rules, and the door’s correction-evidence refusals; caller-supplied observations determine scope validation, a miss grants no allowance, and every decided verdict retains both nullable remedy fields. Non-UTF-8 command bytes with a shell observation remain a miss: the permission-triage hook receives JSON strings and its classifier has no raw-byte rule to inherit. Copied caller inputs and expected outcomes pin parity without linking caller crates; harness decoding, audit silence, rendering and authenticated observation construction remain caller responsibilities.

**Rule (Pure policy)** · `rule:executecommand:policy` The policy module orders door modes as audit, enforce and allow, bounds effective mode by class, loop and registered floor, preserves named transition and evidence refusals, and offers floor-derived absent settings for caller publication under (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:policy-and-binding]`); floor, citation, transition and setting-evidence checks remain distinct so the caller preserves validation order and custody. Mode-transition allowances concern configuration publication only, and gateway admission preserves budget-before-snapshot-before-cooldown precedence with caller-supplied audit and clock observations, saturating arithmetic and actionable remedies; no policy function writes settings, executes commands or changes caller envelopes.

**Rule (Bounded judgement)** · `rule:executecommand:triage` The triage module validates the distinct DoorVerdict and HookSixClass profiles, includes cleanup in the bounded call budget and maps runner outcomes into common verdicts under (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:escalation]`). Invalid output, including a model scope below the observed floor, refers with its own status and never allows an effect. The rubric module supplies exact-byte digests and untrusted-data framing; protected loading and backend invocation remain with the adapters. Escalation answers bind request, context and current review after caller authentication. An unprovisioned external route carries execute-command-route-unprovisioned as its remedy; this is no claim of delivery or operations authority under (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:escalation]`). Existing caller envelopes retain their shapes.

The record boundary supplies review-state and current-review preconditions, completion retry classification, retained decision remedies and the hook audit publication exception. Learning classifies reviewed agreements, corrections and labels after undecided or referred predictions, and preserves an enforced correction’s audit override until a newer setting resolves it. These functions inspect caller-supplied evidence only; request-core remains the common transition engine, and custody owners retain locks and durable publication.

The process module orders reviewed proposal consumption through the ProposalCustody adapter: withdrawal, durable common claim, existing intent recovery or answer application, then completion. A failed claim stops before effect, a published execution intent is recovered without replay, and an interrupted completion retains the same outcome on retry. The adapter supplies authenticated observations and request-core transitions; the library neither opens a journal nor spawns a runner.

The snapshot module carries the owner-published policy observations of (`[TOOLS-rule:commandcontracts:direct-policy]`), their source revisions and canonical observation digests. It rejects unknown envelope shapes and changed observations as execute-command-record-shape. Filesystem custody, effective-UID authentication, bounded reads and publication under the owner store lock remain adapter responsibilities; deserializing a snapshot does not authenticate it.

**Protocol (Compute workspace contracts)** · `proto:executecommand:compute-workspace`

The workspace module is a reader-only operation beside the legacy compute checks. It performs no filesystem access, workspace placement, command execution or caller selection. Transport schema three carries exactly grant_digest, cwd, execution, env_additions and deadline_ms. Cwd is an ordered list of W-relative components with no empty, dot, parent, slash or NUL component. Argv is one to 256 byte words with a nonempty program and no NUL; empty argument values remain representable. Sequence contains one to eight Argv steps, with fixed cwd and environment, one overall deadline and a stopped prefix of per-step outcome, wall and CPU observations. Its continuation function stops on nonzero exit, signal, timeout, unknown outcome or exhausted deadline. Shell carries a grant-named absolute shell and exact UTF-8 text without NUL. Execution content is bounded at 65,536 bytes; cwd and environment each have the same byte bound, and the wire decoder bounds the JSON frame at 1,048,576 bytes. Malformed transport is door-workspace-protocol-refusal, independently of command semantics.

The schema-one grant binds loop, run, node identity, absolute W, ordered baseline environment including PATH, named resource paths, permitted shells, the quick-policy and blacklist digests and executor protocol one. The immutable snapshot is the grant and its canonical digest, stored once in the protected reservation's plan; W1 reads it, and a later launch preview/apply is its only intended producer. The transport's grant_digest is checked against that protected declaration before classification. Missing registration names door-workspace-unregistered; stale or foreign context names door-workspace-context-differs. Existing peer, run, node, scope and SSH-argv authority checks remain the caller's prerequisites and are not policy misses.

Canonical JSON uses explicitly sorted object keys recursively, ordered arrays and compact UTF-8, independently of the JSON map-order feature. The full SHA-256 grant and policy digests bind requester-controlled execution context to the independently published owner snapshot and policy, preventing substitution of another grant or rule table. The execution and learned-context digests bind candidate bytes to the exact request retained by the authenticated decision owner; they confer no authority when supplied only beside their own bytes. The learned key is the class, compute-workspace operation and SHA-256 of canonical grant, policy revision, cwd, execution and ordered environment additions. Legacy compute labels cannot match it.

The positive table contains only exact Argv or Sequence rules, their program resolutions, PATH, every environment addition and declared path-operand slots. A bare program quick-matches only a single absolute PATH directory and its exact declared program; an absolute program must match its declaration exactly. Environment names are nonempty and contain neither equals nor NUL; values contain no NUL. Ordered repeated names are preserved as command content but miss quick matching rather than acquiring an implicit overwrite rule. Multi-directory searches, unknown forms, missing hygiene, late or conditional cleanup and unbound environment changes miss. Path operands are checked lexically at component boundaries against W; a known outside operand misses unless a separately published blacklist entry names the exact execution digest. A blacklist entry matches one whole execution by that digest; it cannot name a path, a cwd, an environment addition or one step of a sequence. An empty or relative PATH remains representable in the owner environment; a bare program whose resolution is not bound by one declared absolute directory misses, while an explicitly bound absolute program need not search PATH. W1 makes no filesystem observation or symlink-proof claim. A blacklist match alone constructs the opaque Deny evidence carrying its published rule, revision and reason; the quick-rule type has no denial field. Shell text never quick-matches, even if a positive table repeats it exactly. QuickAllow evidence is opaque and constructible only by the matcher, bound to the classified execution context. Disposition derives the expected key from the admitted envelope, grant and policy revision and refuses replayed positive evidence; its learned allowance must come from authenticated decision custody, not from the request itself. A published blacklist entry blocks in every mode. Under allow, a quick or learned allowance runs immediately, a miss runs while judgement proceeds, and a learned refusal runs with the refusal recorded. Under enforce, a quick or learned allowance runs immediately, a miss waits for judgement, and a learned refusal blocks. Under audit, every request waits. This is the type contract for the later caller, not a change to the deployed legacy route.

Agent input retains exact byte arrays for Argv and Sequence and exact Unicode Shell text, with cwd, ordered environment additions, the grant summary and miss reasons. Beside those authoritative values it carries an escaped, display-only rendering of each execution word and shell text; a non-UTF-8 word may look different there but its byte array remains exact. Control, format and bidirectional code points, including U+2028 and U+2029 line separators, are listed with their field and byte offset; invalid UTF-8 argument bytes remain bytes and are never replacement characters in the authoritative execution. A rendered input is evidence for judgement, not shell text to execute.

Schema-seven workspace effect evidence contains the envelope, grant, policy and blacklist revisions, execution and context digests, explicit Unknown, Exited, Signalled or TimedOut outcome, nullable wall and CPU measurements, admission wall and per-step sequence evidence. Unknown means unmeasured, never exit zero. A measured aggregate TimedOut may accompany a strictly shorter all-success sequence prefix when the overall deadline expires between steps. The prefix may be empty when no step started. W2b must record every started step, including a step killed by timeout or signal; an empty or shorter successful prefix is permitted only when no omitted step started. An absent aggregate wall, a failed or unknown prefix, or a full successful sequence cannot use this exception; ordinary per-step timeouts still match the last step. Admission wall is separate, so execution wall is not compared with the whole-request deadline. No tree-clean, checkout-commit or implicit cleanup claim is made. The existing schema-six record remains the legacy operation's format. Workspace records are read without conversion by the common reader, explicit rewrite and learned-index rebuild; the historical three/four/five conversion still targets six only. W1 enables no writer or installed route. Once a later stage first writes seven, rollback must retain the readers of W1 and W1b, and earlier tools cannot consume that custody.
